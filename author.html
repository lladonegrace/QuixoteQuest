Author dossier

Miguel de Cervantes Saavedra

Soldier, captive, tax officer, and relentless experimenter whose satire of chivalry turned into the blueprint for the modern novel.

Life in brief

Born September 29, 1547, in Alcalá de Henares to a surgeon father whose unsettled work kept the family moving. He likely studied under Jesuits, then with humanist Juan Lopez de Hoyos in Madrid.

Soldier in the Spanish navy (1570); fought at the Battle of Lepanto (1571) where wounds crippled his left hand. Captured by Ottoman corsairs (1575) and held in Algiers for five years, plotting several failed escapes before ransom freed him in 1580.

Writer-for-hire and tax officer afterward, often in debt. Published La Galatea (1585) with modest notice; turned to theater and short fiction. Don Quixote Part I (1605) made him famous; Part II (1615) answered a fake sequel and sharpened the meta-jokes.

Late output included Novelas ejemplares (1613), the satirical poem Viaje del Parnaso (1614), and the romance Persiles y Sigismunda (1617, posthumous). Cervantes died in Madrid on April 22, 1616; his burial is recorded at the Convent of the Barefoot Trinitarians.

Influence and legacy

  • Helped define the modern novel: layered narrators, self-editing text, and playful doubt about truth.
  • Inspired authors from Fielding and Sterne to Flaubert, Dostoevsky, Borges, and Garcia Marquez.
  • Model for metafiction: the fake sequel inside Part II anticipates copyright, canon, and fanfiction debates.
  • Bridges eras: Renaissance humanism meets Baroque disillusion and early modern skepticism.
